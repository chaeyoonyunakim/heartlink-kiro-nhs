Regression — Maximum Heart Rate Prediction

Linear Regression predicting maximum heart rate (thalch) from age, resting blood pressure, and cholesterol. Eight dataset variants are tested across hospital inclusion, imputation method, and outlier removal.

R² comparison across variants

Key point:

What does this mean? R² measures how much of the variation in maximum heart rate is explained by the model. Higher is better (1.0 = perfect). The best variant uses Cleveland-only data, suggesting that single-source data with consistent collection practices produces more reliable predictions than pooling all hospitals.

Best variant — predicted vs actual

Key point:

What does this mean? Points close to the red dashed line indicate accurate predictions. The scatter shows the model captures the general trend (higher actual = higher predicted) but with substantial spread — age, BP, and cholesterol alone explain only part of maximum heart rate variation.

Key point:

What does this mean? Residuals (prediction errors) should be randomly scattered around zero with no pattern. A roughly even spread here confirms the model's assumptions are reasonable, though some large residuals (±40 bpm) indicate cases where additional features would improve prediction.
